Subject: Beyond Chatbots -- AI-Activity: A Convivial, Collaborative and Cumulative Framework

Dear FOSS Developer Colleagues,

By Convivial, I mean: the user and not the tool maker is in charge.

By Collaborative, I mean: you in many AI sessions and many colleagues working
together towards well defined goals.

By Cumulative, I mean: your colleagues can profit from the details of your AI
interactions and you can profit from the details of your colleagues AI
interactions.

By Framework, I mean: a set of foundational facilities that allow customization
and expansion.

The default mode of AI-assisted development today is *individualistic*. That is
not good. Like most of you, over the past year or so I have been trying to
understand the basic characteristics of the "right" models for team oriented AI
interactions.

I am now ready to share with you what I have come up with. It revolves around
the concept of "AI-Activity". I have formalized it. I use it all day long. It is
not abstract or academic.

The Model (public): https://github.com/bisos-pip/aiActivity
The Tool (public): pipx install bisos.aiActivity
Example Templates (public): https://github.com/bxexamples/aiActivityTemplates


An AI-Activity is a durable, collaborative effort between a human and an AI
assistant (Claude Code) toward a specific purpose - writing a document,
building new software, enhancing existing software, and so on. Unlike a
one-shot chat, an activity is expected to span many sessions, possibly
across machines and over long stretches of time. The AI-Activity is then
expected to be handed off to other team members.

The framework and the model is public and well described at: https://github.com/bisos-pip/aiActivity.

It is structured as:

1) A set of tools available at PyPi
2) A set of templates that we create and maintain

The tool itself =aiActivity.cs= is very simple.

The power of this model will come from the richness of the templates.

At this time, the example AI-Activity templates are very thin. The templates
include Claude commands, skills, definitions of the activities, definition of
our workflow and workplans. These are all well formalized and follow well
defined structures.

Use of this model of collaborative AI-Activity can only become significant if
teams buy into it.

I have been using it for my own projects. I am very happy with it. The model of
AI-Activity is far superior to the default chatbot interactions model.
The example templates are at: https://github.com/bxexamples/aiActivityTemplates

I am not the only one thinking that we should move beyond the chatbot model.
/What if AI systems weren't chatbots?/ is a peer-reviewed argument that
the chatbot paradigm systematically erodes user agency, deskills its
users, and homogenizes knowledge - harms that task-specific,
workflow-integrated AI systems largely avoid. The AI-Activity is my move
toward /convivial tools/ - shaped by their user rather than the reverse -
and away from the industrial default.

The FOSS community already knows well how to collaborate. Linux, Git, and
org-mode are not accidents — they are decades of deliberate choices about what
makes software development cumulative and convivial. The risk with AI is not the
tools themselves but the default mode of individualistic use: each developer in
a separate chatbot session, nothing persisting, nothing shared. AI-Activity is a
different answer: bring AI inside the collaborative substrate you already trust,
shaped by the structures already there. Symlinks into a shared templates tree
mean a convention refined by one team member propagates to every project that
references it. Git tracks the work. Org-mode carries the context. The AI works
within that — not instead of it.
